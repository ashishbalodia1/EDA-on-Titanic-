Data Cleaning & Missing Value Justifications

Systematic handling of missing entries as required by the InternCircle project rubric:

Feature Missing Count Missing % Applied Fix Analytical Rationale
age 177 19.87% Median by (pclass, sex) Age was strongly related to passenger class (1st class median ~36 vs 3rd class median ~22). Group median avoids skewing.
deck 688 77.22% df.drop(columns=['deck']) Over 77% missing. Imputation would create synthetic noise; dropping rows would discard most of the sample.
embarked 2 0.22% Mode ('S' Southampton) Over 72% of passengers embarked at Southampton. Mode resolves missingness without altering proportions.
duplicates 107 12.01% Retained all rows Identical rows are genuine passengers who paid identical fares (names/tickets were removed from this dataset).

Uncovering Hidden Survival Patterns

Deeper multivariate intersections that reveal why aggregate numbers tell only half the story.

01

The Steerage Penalty for Women

Class × Gender Interaction

While women had an overall 74.2% survival rate, upper-class women were almost fully protected, whereas half of steerage women died:

1st Class Female 96.81% (91 / 94)
2nd Class Female 92.11% (70 / 76)
3rd Class Female 50.00% (72 / 144)
Key Insight: 1st-class men (36.89%) survived at nearly triple the rate of 3rd-class men (13.54%), proving wealth partially mitigated the male survival penalty.
02

The Family Evacuation "Sweet Spot"

Non-linear Survival Curve of Family Size

Family size was neither strictly positive nor negative—it followed an inverted U-curve:

Solo Travelers (Size 1): 30.35%

Had no relatives to sound warnings or secure lifeboats.

Small Families (Size 2–4): 57.88% (Sweet Spot)

Peaked at 72.41% for families of 4 due to mutual coordination.

Large Families (Size 5+): 14.75% (Collapse)

Families of 8 and 11 had 0% survival due to crowded steerage bottlenecks.

Key Insight: 1 to 3 companions offered ideal assistance, but coordinating large groups during midnight evacuation was tragic.

04

Embarkation Port & Wealth Proxies

Cherbourg vs Southampton vs Queenstown

Why did passengers from Cherbourg survive at a much higher rate (55.4%) than Southampton (33.9%)?

Cherbourg (C) 55.4% Survived (Mean Fare: £59.95) 50.6% 1st Class
Queenstown (Q) 39.0% Survived (Mean Fare: £13.28) 93.5% 3rd Class
Southampton (S) 33.9% Survived (Mean Fare: £27.24) 63.8% 3rd Class
Key Insight: Port was a direct proxy for wealth; Cherbourg had the highest proportion of wealthy Continental travelers.
